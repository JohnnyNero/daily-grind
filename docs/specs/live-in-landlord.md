# Live-in landlord (potential spec)

**Status: parked.** A possible unique spin, recorded so it isn't lost. It could also pull the game away from its core (the tenants and their stories), so it waits until the minute-to-minute work has landed and been played.

## The idea

You live in the building you own. Your character is a pawn like the tenants, in one of the five flats, with a mood of their own. You do some jobs in person instead of only answering letters and the chat, and the building happens to you too: the party upstairs, the cold, the burglary.

One line: *you live in the block, above the people who pay you.*

## Decided so far

- **Your flat is one of the five.** Living there costs you that flat's rent, which is a real early choice. Moving to a better flat (or the penthouse you build on the roof) is a goal.
- **Your character is customisable:** name and look, like the tenants are drawn.

## Why it's attractive

- **A direct verb.** There's always something you could be doing in person, and you choose what. It fits the drip: many cards can offer "go yourself" against "pay someone".
- **It's personal.** The building's noise, warmth, dirt and neighbours affect you, so a ruthless landlord living over a resentful building is its own story.
- **Two-way relationships.** Tenants judge you on what you do on the stairs as well as in letters. Gossip about you reaches someone you meet in the lobby.
- **Early money and choices.** Doing jobs yourself saves money and costs time, energy and mood.
- **It fits the bible:** few people who matter (you're one of them), stories over spreadsheets, nice or ruthless both playable.

## The risks

- **Puppeteering.** If you steer every step, it breaks "landlord, not puppeteer". Answer: jobs, not steps. You tap a job and your character goes and does it.
- **It competes with the drip** for attention. Keep the job list short, and most cards keep a "pay someone" option.
- **Needs as a chore.** Your needs must be lighter than a tenant's: energy and mood (maybe hunger), with your flat setting how well you recover.
- **Pace.** While your character is busy, other cards must wait or offer "pay someone". No time tricks: the speed buttons stay the only pace control.
- **Scope.** It touches the handover, the economy, opinions, events, the UI (a "You" tab, a job list) and saves: a milestone the size of the events work.
- **Mood without a spiral.** A tired landlord makes worse calls (snaps at a tenant, botches a repair). That's drama, never a debuff spiral: your mood is a ledger of labelled thoughts like everyone's.

## How it might work

1. **You as a pawn** in one of the five flats, holding every key. A "You" tab shows your mood, thoughts, skills and jobs.
2. **Jobs:** collect rent, fix something, show a viewing, check on a tenant, do the stairs, chase arrears. Cards offer "do it myself" (free, takes time, your mood and skill matter) or "pay someone" (money, done).
3. **Your mood** comes from labelled thoughts (your flat, the noise, how the week went, what tenants said to you) and shapes how jobs go: a cheerful landlord charms, a knackered one rows.
4. **Skills that grow with use:** DIY (cheaper, better repairs) and charm (better haggles, calmer complaints). Growth for you, not just the building.
5. **Endings:** if the bank repossesses the building, you lose your home too.

## Viability

High. About 80% exists already: pawns, needs, thoughts, pathing and keys (you'd hold all of them, like Denise), opinions, and step-by-step jobs (the burglar, the handover walk). New: the job list and its UI, your character's link into money and the cards, and a light needs model.

## Open questions

- Should "pay someone" always be available, so a player can stay hands-off?
- Which flat do you start in: the worst one (a reason to do it up), or your choice?
- Do tenants know you live there from day one, and does that change who applies?
- What does your character do when you're not giving them a job: their own life, like a tenant?
- How does it sit with the drip: does living in reduce how many cards need you, or add "go yourself" options to existing ones?
